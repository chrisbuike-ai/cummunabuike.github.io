Reflection on Spiritual-Professional Integration

As I approach the culmination of my degree, I have created a "Mental Map" to visualize how my faith and my IT career intersect, and how I will maintain that alignment throughout my professional life.

The Intersection: Data as Stewardship

At the center of my mental map is the concept of stewardship. In IT, we are entrusted with the digital footprints, financial data, and personal identities of God’s children. Treating this data with integrity, security, and ethical foresight is not just a technical requirement; it is a modern expression of loving our neighbor. My professional skills (cloud architecture, SQL, cybersecurity) are the tools I use to fulfill this spiritual stewardship.

Personal Warning Signs of Divergence

Through deep reflection, I have identified three personal warning signs that indicate my spiritual beliefs and professional practices are beginning to diverge:

  • The "Efficiency Trap" (Cutting Corners): When I prioritize speed and convenience over doing the work correctly. If I find myself skipping documentation, bypassing security checks, or accepting a "good enough" data model, I am sacrificing integrity for efficiency.
  • Data Detachment: When I begin to view the rows and columns in a database merely as abstract numbers rather than real people with real lives. This detachment is the first step toward unethical data practices.
  • Technical Arrogance (The "Empty Microwave"): When I rely solely on my own intellect and stop seeking spiritual guidance. As Elder Johnson taught, if I am not filled with the word of God, the "fiery darts" of stress and pride will consume me, leading to burnout and poor ethical decisions.

Strategies for Realignment

To counteract these warning signs, I have developed the following strategies to maintain my alignment:

  • The Disciple-Leader Pause: Before tackling a complex technical problem or making a critical architectural decision, I will pause to pray. This invites revelation and ensures my intellect is harmonized with the Spirit.
  • Sabbath Unplugging: I will strictly observe the Sabbath as a digital detox. Stepping away from screens and code allows my soul to be replenished, preventing the "empty microwave" effect and keeping my eternal perspective sharp.
  • Asking the Three Questions: When faced with an ethical dilemma at work, I will ask Elder Clark’s disciple-leader questions: What is my purpose? How can I lift others? How can I add value? This ensures my technical decisions always serve a higher, Christlike standard.
